Sign permitting guide: Pasco, Pinellas & Hillsborough County

Updated October 2026 · Fly Visuals · 10 min read

What actually needs a permit, who reviews it, and what we've found in Pasco, Pinellas and Hillsborough County, from a sign shop that files these applications every week.

Why there's no one answer to “Do I need a sign permit?”

Florida doesn't have one state office that issues sign permits. The Florida Building Code applies statewide. It sets the structural wind-load and electrical standards every sign has to meet. But the permit application, review and approval happen at the county or city building department, along with that area's own zoning and sign code for size, height and placement. A sign that's exempt in one city can need a full permit two miles away.

This guide explains the general pattern across most Florida cities, what we've found in Port Richey and New Port Richey, and where you need to call a building department to confirm current fees and size limits.

This is general information based on our research and experience, not legal advice. Sign codes change. The only way to be sure about a specific address is to confirm with that building department before the sign is made, which is exactly what we do when we handle your permit.

The short version

  • Temporary sign (real estate, event or a short-run banner): often exempt or needs a simple temporary permit. Confirm the size and time limit locally first.
  • New permanent sign (wall, monument or pole): a building permit is required, with sealed engineering for anything structural.
  • Lit sign (channel letters or backlit): needs a building permit and a separate electrical permit, pulled by a licensed electrical contractor or a dual-licensed sign contractor.

[CITY A: Port Richey or New Port Richey] sign permit specifics

This city publishes real numbers in its sign permit application, which is more than most do:

  • Maximum sign area: 1.75 sq ft per linear foot of building frontage, or 0.5 sq ft per linear foot of lot frontage, whichever is larger.
  • Overall cap: 200 sq ft for a single face; 32 sq ft minimum.
  • Freestanding signs need a 15-foot setback from the property line.
  • Sealed engineering plans are required, showing compliance with Florida's 140–150 mph wind-load standard.
  • A Notice of Commencement is required if the job costs more than $2,500.
  • The permit becomes void if work doesn't start within 6 months.

[CITY B: Port Richey or New Port Richey] sign permit specifics

This city's checklist doesn't publish size or fee figures, but it does spell out the process:

  • An owner-permission letter is required if the applicant isn't the building owner.
  • A Notice of Commencement is required above $5,000.
  • Lit signs need a separate “Electrical Final” inspection on top of the building permit sign-off.
  • Signs are allowed in commercial zoning per the city's checklist.

Pasco, Pinellas and Hillsborough County

Commercial sign permits are required in all three counties, and the Florida Building Code's structural and wind-load rules apply everywhere. What we couldn't find were published county fee schedules, size caps or turnaround times for unincorporated areas. Those are confirmed directly with each county:

  • Pasco County: Building Construction Services, (727) 847-8126 option 5, or the PascoGateway online permitting portal.
  • Pinellas County: Building & Development Review Services, (727) 464-3000. Pinellas County's general permit threshold applies to any work over $500.
  • Hillsborough County: Building Services, which runs the Florida Building Code locally along with the county's flood rules.

When we handle your permit, confirming the current fee, size limit and timeline with that department is part of the job. You don't have to make these calls.

Lit signs need an electrical permit too

Any sign with electrical parts (channel letters, backlit acrylic, a lit cabinet) has its electrical work reviewed against the National Electrical Code. That means a separate electrical permit on top of the sign permit. The work has to be done by a licensed electrical contractor, or by a contractor with Florida's Sign Electrical (ES) license, which lets a sign company do both the sign and the electrical under one license.

A recent change worth knowing: as of July 1, 2024, Florida amended the ES license statute (F.S. 489.505) to also cover non-electrical sign work. An ES-licensed sign company can now handle the full sign job without bringing in a separate general contractor.

What this means for you: If you're planning a lit sign, plan for two inspections instead of one, and make sure whoever pulls your permit holds the right license for the electrical part, not just a general contracting license.

Mistakes that cost Pasco County customers money

  • Installing before the permit is approved. An unpermitted sign can mean a stop-work order, removal and fines on top of the permit fee you tried to avoid.
  • Assuming the next city's rules are the same. Port Richey and New Port Richey are a few miles apart and have different requirements. Always confirm for the exact address.
  • Hiring an unlicensed installer for a lit sign. It's a compliance risk and a real electrical safety issue.
  • Not budgeting for a Notice of Commencement. One city requires it above $2,500, the other above $5,000. Plan for it in your timeline.
  • Assuming every temporary banner is exempt. Size and time limits vary and aren't always published. Confirm before you print.

Sign permit questions

Do I need a permit for a grand opening banner?

It depends on the city or county and how long the banner will be up. Many Florida cities exempt temporary banners under a certain size or time, but the limit isn't the same everywhere. We'll confirm the rule for your address before you print. If a temporary permit is needed, it's usually simpler and faster than a permanent sign permit.

Does Fly Visuals pull the permit for me?

Yes. On any job that needs a permit, we handle the application and submission. Fees are billed at cost with no markup.

What if my sign is lit?

A lit sign needs its electrical work reviewed separately, which means a second permit and a second inspection. We include both when we quote a lit sign.

How long does permit approval take?

It depends on the department and its workload. We didn't find a published turnaround time for Pasco, Pinellas or Hillsborough County, and we'd rather say that than guess. When we submit, we'll give you a realistic estimate and keep you updated.

Do real estate or yard signs need a permit?

They're often exempt below a size limit, but not everywhere. If you order yard signs from us, we'll flag anything your area requires.
