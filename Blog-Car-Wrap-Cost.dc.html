1. Size of the vehicle

More panels means more vinyl and more labor. A small coupe uses far less material than a crew cab truck or a cargo van.

2. How much you cover

A full wrap covers every painted panel. A partial wrap covers part of the vehicle and blends into the paint, which can cut the cost a lot while still looking great.

3. The film you choose

Standard gloss and satin colors cost less than specialty films like color shift, chrome or textured finishes. We use premium cast vinyl from 3M and Avery that is made for curves and Florida heat.

4. Printed designs vs. solid colors

Business wraps are printed and laminated, and they include design time. Solid color changes use colored film, so there is no printing, but more careful panel work.

5. Door jambs and extras

Wrapping door jambs makes a color change look finished when the doors are open, but it adds time. Chrome delete, roof wraps and tinted lights can be added to any package.

Tip: Ask about pairing your wrap with ceramic tint or paint protection film on the front end. Doing them in one visit saves you time.

Is a wrap cheaper than paint?

For a quality job, a color change wrap usually costs less than a comparable paint job. It also protects the factory paint and can be removed later, which helps resale value.
